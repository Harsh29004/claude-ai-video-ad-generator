# Compliance Checklist (claims, synthetic actors, disclosure)

This is practical guidance, not legal advice. For regulated categories (health, finance, alcohol, kids, gambling) tell the user to get a legal or compliance review before launch.

## Why this matters
A realistic AI person in an ad is persuasive *because* viewers read them as real. That's the point of the creative, and it's also where ads become deceptive. Regulators and ad platforms now police this specifically, and a rejected or taken-down ad wastes the whole production spend.

## Before you hand off, confirm each item

**Claims**
- [ ] Every feature, number, rating, price and comparison in copy and video traces to a source in `01-product-brief.md`
- [ ] No invented stats ("10x faster", "1M users"), ratings or awards
- [ ] Comparative claims about competitors are factual and sourced, or removed
- [ ] Health, finance and earnings outcomes are not promised ("lose 10 kg", "earn ₹50k"), only what the product does

**Synthetic people**
- [ ] All characters are fictional. No real person's or celebrity's name, likeness or voice, and no "looks like <famous person>" prompts
- [ ] Any real person's photo used for image-to-video has that person's confirmed consent
- [ ] Characters dramatize a scenario. They don't pose as real customers giving testimonials or reviews. The US FTC's rule on consumer reviews and testimonials (16 CFR Part 465, in force since Oct 2024) explicitly covers AI-generated fake testimonials, with civil penalties per violation
- [ ] No minors as characters unless the user specifically needs it and the platform allows it; prefer adults

**Disclosure**
- [ ] The on-screen label is present (default "AI-generated dramatization"). New York's synthetic-performer ad disclosure law (in effect June 2026) requires a conspicuous disclosure when an ad features a synthetic performer, and other states are following
- [ ] Platform AI labels are set at upload: Meta "AI info", TikTok's AI-generated content label, YouTube's altered or synthetic content disclosure. Mention this in the handoff, because the user toggles it when uploading

**Research ethics**
- [ ] No real usernames, handles or verbatim posts from Phase 2 appear in the ad
- [ ] Timely hooks don't exploit tragedies, disasters or protected characteristics

If the user asks to drop the disclosure or to present characters as real customers, explain the risk briefly and offer a compliant alternative: a clearly framed dramatization, or real customer testimonials gathered with consent.
